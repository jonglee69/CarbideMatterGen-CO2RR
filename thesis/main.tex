\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The electrochemical reduction of carbon dioxide to carbon monoxide (CO\textsubscript{2}RR-to-CO)
is a key step toward closing the anthropogenic carbon cycle, but the most selective known
catalysts (Au, Ag) are scarce and expensive. This thesis develops and applies an end-to-end
\emph{generative} discovery pipeline that searches directly for \emph{economical} CO-evolving
catalysts within the chemical space of high-entropy transition-metal carbides (HECs). A
property-conditioned diffusion model (a fine-tuned \textsc{MatterGen}) is steered toward
stable, metallic, CO-releasing and platinum-group-metal-free compositions; the generated pool
is funneled through composition proxies, a machine-learned interatomic-potential (UMA)
surface free-energy screen, and density-functional-theory (DFT) validation in
\textsc{Quantum ESPRESSO}. Starting from 1024 generated structures the pipeline converges on
six candidate HECs (Pr\textsubscript{3}Y\textsubscript{2}WC\textsubscript{7},
La\textsubscript{5}Y\textsubscript{4}W\textsubscript{2}C\textsubscript{9},
Ce\textsubscript{2}Y\textsubscript{2}ZrWC\textsubscript{6},
La\textsubscript{3}Y\textsubscript{5}(WC\textsubscript{3})\textsubscript{3},
Y\textsubscript{2}ZrWC\textsubscript{8},
CeY\textsubscript{2}ZrWC\textsubscript{7}), each predicted to host a near-thermoneutral CO
site ($|\dGCO|\lesssim\SI{0.02}{eV}$) and CO-over-H selectivity. DFT confirms all six are
metallic; projected densities of states resolve the conduction character as a carbon-$2p$
network hybridized with the metal $d$ sublattice. A complementary experimental validation
campaign---materials synthesis, electrochemical CO\textsubscript{2}RR testing, on-line
gas analysis for CO/CO\textsubscript{2}, \textsuperscript{1}H NMR for liquid products, and
(in-situ) FT-IR for adsorbed intermediates---is designed and presented; the corresponding
results are reserved for completion. The work demonstrates that economic constraints can be
embedded \emph{at the generative stage}, shifting catalyst discovery from screening known
materials toward designing affordable new ones.
\par\vspace{6pt}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} CO\textsubscript{2} reduction; high-entropy carbides; generative
models; machine-learned interatomic potentials; density functional theory; non-precious
electrocatalysts.
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

\section{Motivation: affordable CO\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{2}-to-CO conversion}
Electrochemical CO\textsubscript{2} reduction (CO\textsubscript{2}RR) powered by renewable
electricity offers a route to convert a waste greenhouse gas into chemical feedstocks. Among
the accessible products, carbon monoxide is one of the most technologically valuable: it is the
two-electron product with the highest demonstrated selectivity and is the entry point to
Fischer--Tropsch and carbonylation chemistry. The selectivity of a metal electrode for CO is
governed by how strongly it binds the key intermediates \mbox{*COOH} and *CO relative to
adsorbed hydrogen *H; the classic survey of metal electrodes by Hori established that Au, Ag and
Zn are the principal CO-evolving metals, whereas Cu uniquely proceeds to hydrocarbons and most
other metals favor the competing hydrogen-evolution reaction (HER)~\cite{hori2008}. The
mechanistic basis of this behavior on copper, and the central role of the *COOH and *CO
descriptors, is reviewed comprehensively by Nitopi \emph{et al.}~\cite{nitopi2019}.

The difficulty is economic rather than purely chemical: the most CO-selective metals (Au, Ag)
are scarce and costly, which is incompatible with the terawatt scale at which CO\textsubscript{2}
conversion would need to operate to matter for climate. There is therefore a pressing need for
catalysts that reproduce the favorable adsorption-energy landscape of the coinage metals using
earth-abundant elements.

\section{Why high-entropy carbides}
Two materials-design ideas are combined in this thesis. First, transition-metal carbides (TMCs)
have long been known to display ``platinum-like'' surface chemistry: the seminal observation of
Levy and Boudart that tungsten carbide mimics platinum in several catalytic
reactions~\cite{levy1973} initiated decades of work on TMCs as inexpensive substitutes for
precious metals, and molybdenum carbide in particular is an established, selective catalyst for
reducing CO\textsubscript{2} to CO~\cite{porosoff2014}. Carbon donates electron density into the
metal $d$ band and reshapes the surface density of states, tuning adsorption strengths into
useful windows.

Second, \emph{high-entropy} materials---near-equimolar solid solutions of four or more
elements---provide a near-continuous distribution of local adsorption environments. Batchelor
\emph{et al.}\ argued that this continuum is precisely what an electrocatalyst needs, because a
small fraction of optimally-binding sites can dominate the measured
activity~\cite{batchelor2019}; high-entropy alloys have since been realized experimentally as
CO\textsubscript{2}/CO reduction catalysts~\cite{nellaiappan2020}. High-entropy carbides are
synthesizable single-phase materials in their own right, as demonstrated by the
entropy-descriptor discovery of refractory five-metal carbides~\cite{sarker2018}. Combining the
carbide ``platinum-like'' electronic structure with high-entropy site diversity---while
restricting the palette to cheap metals---defines the search space of this work.

\section{Why a generative pipeline}
Conventional computational catalyst discovery screens \emph{existing} databases. The combinatorial
space of multi-metal carbides is far too large to enumerate, and the affordable subset is sparse
within it. We instead adopt a \emph{generative} strategy: a diffusion model that has learned the
distribution of stable inorganic crystals~\cite{zeni2025} is fine-tuned so that its sampling is
\emph{steered} toward the target property values---including an explicit \emph{economic}
constraint that penalizes platinum-group and heavy-rare-earth content. Affordability thus becomes
a generation objective rather than a post-hoc filter. Because the model proposes novel structures,
the expensive part of discovery (DFT) is reserved for a handful of already-promising candidates,
reached through a funnel of increasingly accurate but increasingly costly evaluators.

\section{Objectives and structure}
The objectives of this thesis are: (i) to construct an economic-constrained generative pipeline
for CO\textsubscript{2}RR-to-CO HEC catalysts; (ii) to validate the top candidates with
first-principles DFT for stability and metallic conductivity, resolving the orbital character of
the conduction states; and (iii) to design the experimental campaign that will confirm the
computational predictions. Chapter~\ref{ch:theory} reviews the theoretical background;
Chapter~\ref{ch:methods} details the computational methodology; Chapter~\ref{ch:results} presents
the computational results; Chapter~\ref{ch:experiment} sets out the planned experimental
validation; Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} discusses the findings and limitations; and
Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} concludes.

\chapter{Theoretical Background}
\label{ch:theory}

\section{Thermodynamics of CO\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{2}-to-CO and the computational hydrogen electrode}
The two-electron pathway to CO proceeds through a single carboxyl intermediate,
\begin{align}
\mathrm{CO_2(g)} + \mathrm{H^+} + e^- + {*} &\rightarrow \mathrm{*COOH},\\
\mathrm{*COOH} + \mathrm{H^+} + e^- &\rightarrow \mathrm{*CO} + \mathrm{H_2O},\\
\mathrm{*CO} &\rightarrow \mathrm{CO(g)} + {*}.
\end{align}
Reaction free energies of the electrochemical steps are evaluated with the computational
hydrogen electrode (CHE) of N\o{}rskov \emph{et al.}~\cite{norskov2004}, in which the chemical
potential of a proton--electron pair is referenced to gas-phase H\textsubscript{2} at
\SI{0}{V} versus the reversible hydrogen electrode:
\begin{equation}
\mu(\mathrm{H^+}) + \mu(e^-) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{H_2}) - eU .
\end{equation}
The adsorption free energies used throughout are
\begin{align}
\dGCO   &= E(\mathrm{*CO})   - E(*) - E(\mathrm{CO_{(g)}}) + \Delta_{\mathrm{CO}},\\
\dGCOOH &= E(\mathrm{*COOH}) - E(*) - E(\mathrm{CO_{2(g)}}) - \tfrac12 E(\mathrm{H_{2(g)}}) + \Delta_{\mathrm{COOH}},\\
\dGH    &= E(\mathrm{*H})    - E(*) - \tfrac12 E(\mathrm{H_{2(g)}}) + \Delta_{\mathrm{H}},
\end{align}
where the $\Delta$ terms are standard zero-point and entropic corrections. A good CO-evolving
site requires $\dGCO$ near zero (CO is released as product rather than poisoning the surface),
$\dGCOOH$ low enough to activate CO\textsubscript{2}, and---crucially for selectivity---%
$\dGCOOH < \dGH$, so that CO\textsubscript{2}RR out-competes HER.

\section{Descriptors, the Sabatier principle and carbides}
Because adsorption energies on related surfaces scale with one another, a single descriptor
(here the best-site $\dGCO$) captures most of the activity trend, and the optimum is a Sabatier
maximum at intermediate binding. In carbides, carbon $p$ states hybridize with metal $d$ states
and shift the $d$-band, which is the microscopic origin of the ``platinum-like'' behavior of
WC~\cite{levy1973} and of the CO selectivity of Mo\textsubscript{2}C~\cite{porosoff2014}. A
metallic density of states at the Fermi level is a prerequisite for an electrocatalyst, so
verifying metallicity by DFT is an essential validation step.

\section{Generative diffusion models for crystals}
\textsc{MatterGen}~\cite{zeni2025} is a diffusion model that jointly denoises the atomic
coordinates, atom types and lattice of a crystal, trained on a large database of stable
inorganic compounds. Conditioning is achieved by fine-tuning property-embedding adapters so that
classifier-free guidance biases sampling toward target values of one or more properties; the
guidance strength $w$ trades diversity against on-target yield. This is the mechanism we exploit
to embed both catalytic and \emph{economic} targets directly into generation.

\section{Machine-learned interatomic potentials}
Evaluating surface free energies for thousands of candidates by DFT is intractable. We use a
universal machine-learned interatomic potential (MLIP), the Universal Model for Atoms
(UMA)~\cite{uma2025}, whose catalysis behavior derives from training on the Open Catalyst 2020
dataset~\cite{chanussot2021}. The MLIP provides near-DFT adsorption energetics at a tiny fraction
of the cost, serving as the intermediate rung between cheap composition proxies and full DFT.

\section{Density functional theory and pseudopotentials}
Final validation uses Kohn--Sham DFT with the PBE exchange--correlation
functional~\cite{pbe1996} as implemented in the plane-wave code
\textsc{Quantum ESPRESSO}~\cite{giannozzi2009}, with pseudopotentials from the Standard
Solid-State Pseudopotential (SSSP) library~\cite{prandini2018}. Configurational disorder of the
high-entropy sublattice is represented, where required, by special quasirandom structures
(SQS)~\cite{zunger1990}.

\chapter{Computational Methodology}
\label{ch:methods}

\noindent The discovery pipeline is a five-stage funnel of increasing fidelity and cost
(Fig.~\ref{fig:summary}a). Each stage is described below.

\section{Stage A --- economic-conditioned generation}
A base \textsc{MatterGen} checkpoint was fine-tuned with nine conditioning properties: stability
(energy above hull), metallicity (driving the band gap to zero), the primary CO-release
descriptors (best-site CO affinity and the fraction of CO-selective sites), CO\textsubscript{2}
activation (mean *COOH affinity), CO-vs-H selectivity, high mixing entropy, a metallic-packing
proxy, and---decisively---the \emph{expensive-metal fraction}, defined as the fraction of metal
atoms that are platinum-group (Ru, Rh, Pd, Os, Ir, Pt) or heavy rare-earth, which is driven to
zero. Training labels were computed for every transition-metal carbide in the
\texttt{alex\_mp\_20} dataset by literature-informed composition proxies. Fine-tuning ran for 600
epochs; the validation loss is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:loss}. Conditional generation was performed
at two guidance strengths ($w=1.0$ and $w=2.5$), yielding 1024 candidate structures.

\section{Stage B --- cheap composition screen}
Every generated structure was re-evaluated with the composition proxies and filtered to retain
those that are (i) genuine transition-metal carbides, (ii) within the CO-release window, (iii)
high-entropy ($n_\mathrm{metal}\ge 3$, mixing entropy $\ge\!1.0\,k_\mathrm{B}$), and (iv)
economically clean (zero expensive-metal fraction). This narrowed the pool to 104 survivors
(90 distinct compositions).

\section{Stage C --- MLIP surface free-energy screen}
For the 24 highest-ranked distinct compositions, low-index surface terminations were enumerated
(maximum Miller index 1); the most stable termination per facet was relaxed with the bottom half
fixed, each adsorbate (*CO, *COOH, *H) was placed at every symmetry-distinct site, and the
adslabs were relaxed with the UMA potential~\cite{uma2025}. Free energies followed the CHE
expressions of Section~\ref{ch:theory}. Gas-phase and clean-slab references were computed
consistently with the total-energy head of the same potential so that the slab contribution
cancels; non-physical, non-converged sites ($|\Delta G|>\SI{10}{eV}$) were discarded. Each
candidate's descriptors aggregate over \emph{all} computed facets and sites, and the candidates
were ranked by proximity of their best site to the CO optimum and by CO-selective site density.

\section{Stage D --- DFT validation and electronic structure}
The six top-ranked candidates were validated with \textsc{Quantum ESPRESSO}~\cite{giannozzi2009}:
variable-cell relaxation, followed by a self-consistent calculation on the relaxed geometry, and
a density-of-states (DOS) calculation. Plane-wave cutoffs of \SI{60}{Ry} (wavefunctions) and
\SI{480}{Ry} (charge density) were used with Marzari--Vanderbilt cold smearing
(\SI{0.02}{Ry}) and Monkhorst--Pack $k$-meshes of density $\sim\!\SI{30}{\angstrom}/a$. SSSP
pseudopotentials~\cite{prandini2018} were used for C, Y, Zr, W; for the rare earths
(La, Ce, Pr) norm-conserving pseudopotentials with the $4f$ shell frozen in the core
(trivalent) were used, which is physically appropriate for the localized $4f$ states of these
metallic carbides. Element-projected DOS was obtained by projecting the converged Kohn--Sham
states onto atomic orbitals (no additional self-consistency), summed per element to quantify each
sublattice's contribution to the states at the Fermi level.

\section{Stage A$'$ --- solid-solution supercells}
To represent explicit chemical disorder, special quasirandom structures~\cite{zunger1990} of the
high-entropy shortlist were built on rock-salt (MC) or hexagonal (M\textsubscript{2}C) frameworks
by Monte-Carlo matching of the metal-sublattice pair-correlation functions; these supercells are
the substrate for future explicit-disorder surface studies.

\section{Software}
Structure manipulation used \texttt{pymatgen}~\cite{pymatgen2013} and the Atomic Simulation
Environment~\cite{ase2017}; the MLIP screen used the \texttt{fairchem}/UMA
implementation~\cite{uma2025}.

\chapter{Computational Results}
\label{ch:results}

\section{Generation and the discovery funnel}
Fine-tuning converged smoothly (Fig.~\ref{fig:loss}), and conditional generation produced 1024
structures of which 1004 are transition-metal carbides. Their composition-descriptor
distributions (Fig.~\ref{fig:genstats}) show that the economic conditioning was effective: the
expensive-metal fraction is essentially zero across the pool, and the metal palette is dominated
by inexpensive early transition metals and light rare earths (W, Y, Zr, Ce, La, Pr). The cheap
screen retained 104 economical CO-releasing high-entropy carbides. The full funnel
($1024 \rightarrow 1004 \rightarrow 90 \rightarrow 24 \rightarrow 6$) is summarized in
Fig.~\ref{fig:summary}a.

\section{MLIP surface screen}
The UMA surface screen evaluated all low-index facets of the 24 candidates
(Fig.~\ref{fig:surface}). The best-site $\dGCO$ of the leading candidates sits within a few tens
of meV of the CO optimum, and the best site is found on a variety of facets rather than a single
preferred plane---consistent with the high-entropy design intent of distributing favorable sites.
The six top-ranked candidates, all CO-over-H selective at their best site, are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:master}.

\section{DFT validation: stability and metallicity}
All six candidates relaxed to low residual pressure ($|P|<\SI{0.5}{kbar}$) and are
\emph{metallic}, with a finite density of states at the Fermi level ranging from
\SIrange{1.7}{12.4}{states/eV} per cell (Fig.~\ref{fig:dos}, Table~\ref{tab:master}). Metallic
conductivity, a prerequisite for electrocatalysis, is therefore confirmed by first principles.

\section{Conduction character from projected DOS}
The element-projected DOS (Fig.~\ref{fig:pdos}) resolves \emph{which} sublattice carries the
Fermi-level states. Two regimes emerge: carbon-rich candidates
(Pr\textsubscript{3}Y\textsubscript{2}WC\textsubscript{7},
Ce\textsubscript{2}Y\textsubscript{2}ZrWC\textsubscript{6}) are dominated by carbon-$2p$
character at $\Ef$ ($\sim$60\%), describing a conductive carbide network, whereas the
tungsten-rich La\textsubscript{3}Y\textsubscript{5}(WC\textsubscript{3})\textsubscript{3} and the
rare-earth-free Y\textsubscript{2}ZrWC\textsubscript{8} are metal-$d$ dominated (Y/La/W).
The per-element shares are plotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:summary}d. This hybridized C-$p$/metal-$d$
conduction is exactly the electronic signature expected for ``platinum-like'' carbide
catalysts~\cite{levy1973}.

\chapter{Planned Experimental Validation}
\label{ch:experiment}

\noindent The computational pipeline yields concrete, falsifiable predictions: the six candidates
should (i) be synthesizable as single-phase metallic carbides and (ii) electroreduce
CO\textsubscript{2} to CO with selectivity exceeding HER. This chapter specifies the experimental
program designed to test them. Results are reserved until the campaign is complete.

\section{Materials synthesis}
The shortlisted high-entropy carbides will be synthesized by high-temperature carburization of
ball-milled metal/oxide precursors (or by arc-melting followed by carburization), targeting the
SQS-predicted near-equimolar metal sublattice. Phase purity and the single-phase rock-salt/
hexagonal structure will be verified by powder X-ray diffraction (XRD); homogeneity of the metal
distribution by SEM/EDS and STEM-EDS mapping; and surface composition/oxidation state by X-ray
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). \tbu{Synthesis and structural characterization}

\section{Electrochemical CO\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{2}RR testing}
Catalyst inks will be drop-cast onto gas-diffusion or glassy-carbon electrodes and tested in a
gas-tight H-cell (and an MEA/flow cell for higher current density) in CO\textsubscript{2}-
saturated aqueous bicarbonate electrolyte. Linear-sweep voltammetry and chronoamperometry at a
series of applied potentials will quantify the partial current densities; the geometric and
electrochemically-active-surface-area-normalized activities will be reported. \tbu{Polarization
curves and Faradaic-efficiency vs.\ potential}

\section{Product analysis}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Gas products (CO, H\textsubscript{2}, residual CO\textsubscript{2}).} The cell
headspace/outlet will be sampled on-line into a gas chromatograph (TCD + FID with methanizer) to
quantify CO and H\textsubscript{2} and thus the CO Faradaic efficiency and the CO/H\textsubscript{2}
selectivity; CO\textsubscript{2} conversion will be tracked from the inlet/outlet balance. On-line
differential electrochemical mass spectrometry (DEMS) will provide potential-resolved CO/
H\textsubscript{2} onset. \tbu{GC/DEMS gas-product quantification}
\item \textbf{Liquid products.} The catholyte will be analyzed by quantitative \textsuperscript{1}H
nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy with water suppression and an internal standard
(e.g.\ DMSO/DSS) to detect and quantify any formate or minor liquid products, confirming the
expected dominance of the two-electron CO pathway. \tbu{\textsuperscript{1}H NMR liquid-product
quantification}
\item \textbf{Adsorbed intermediates.} In-situ attenuated-total-reflection surface-enhanced
infrared absorption spectroscopy (ATR-SEIRAS / FT-IR) will be used to detect the *COOH and
linearly/bridge-bound *CO bands as a function of potential, providing a direct mechanistic link
to the computed $\dGCOOH$ and $\dGCO$ descriptors. \tbu{In-situ FT-IR of *COOH/*CO intermediates}
\end{itemize}

\section{Stability and post-mortem analysis}
Extended electrolysis will assess durability; XRD, XPS and microscopy before/after testing will
probe surface oxidation, leaching of the rare-earth components, and carbide-network integrity.
\tbu{Durability and post-mortem characterization}

\section{Validation logic}
The experiments are designed to test the computational predictions point-by-point:
metallic conductivity (four-point probe / electrochemical impedance) against the DFT DOS; CO
selectivity (GC/NMR) against the computed $\dGCO$ and $\dGCOOH<\dGH$ selectivity criterion; and
the mechanistic intermediates (FT-IR) against the descriptor ladder. A predicted-vs-measured

\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{Why this pipeline, and what it buys}
The central methodological claim of this thesis is that \emph{economic constraints belong at the
generative stage}. By fine-tuning the diffusion model on an expensive-metal-fraction label and
guiding it to zero, the affordable subspace is sampled \emph{by construction}, rather than being
discovered by filtering an unconstrained pool in which it is sparse. The resulting funnel spends
DFT only on six structures while still surveying $\sim$10\textsuperscript{3} candidates, with each
rung (composition proxy $\rightarrow$ MLIP $\rightarrow$ DFT) adding accuracy where the previous
rung has already removed the bulk of the search space. This is a generalizable template: swapping
the conditioning descriptors retargets the same machinery to a different reaction.

\section{Interpreting the candidates}
All six candidates combine cheap early transition metals (Y, Zr, W) with light rare earths (La,
Ce, Pr) on the metal sublattice of a carbide. The DFT-resolved conduction character
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pdos}) shows a continuum from carbon-$2p$-network metals to metal-$d$ metals,
mirroring the ``platinum-like'' picture in which carbon reshapes the metal $d$
states~\cite{levy1973}. The near-thermoneutral $\dGCO$ and the $\dGCOOH<\dGH$ selectivity place
these materials in the same descriptor window that makes Au/Ag/Zn CO-selective~\cite{hori2008},
but with earth-abundant constituents---which is precisely the design goal. The success of
high-entropy alloys as experimental CO\textsubscript{2}/CO reduction
catalysts~\cite{nellaiappan2020,batchelor2019} and of Mo\textsubscript{2}C for selective
CO\textsubscript{2}-to-CO~\cite{porosoff2014} lends external plausibility to the carbide+HEC route.

\section{Limitations and honest caveats}
Several caveats must be stated. (i) The light rare earths (La, Ce, Pr, Y) are economically
``inexpensive'' by the chosen descriptor, but rare-earth carbides are unusual CO\textsubscript{2}RR
catalysts and may be oxophilic and air/moisture sensitive; experimental robustness is an open
question that the planned stability tests directly address. (ii) The MLIP surface screen, although
calibrated to cancel reference errors, evaluates rare-earth-carbide surfaces that lie outside the
bulk of its training distribution; the strongly-bound *COOH energies in particular should be read
qualitatively, and the activity ranking leans on the more robust $\dGCO$ descriptor. (iii) The
composition proxies used in Stages A--B are literature-informed estimates intended for coarse
filtering, not quantitative prediction. (iv) The DFT treats the rare-earth $4f$ electrons as
frozen trivalent cores; while appropriate for these metallic carbides, $4f$-in-valence or
DFT$+U$ treatments would be needed for any property sensitive to $f$-states. (v) The present DFT
validates \emph{bulk} stability and metallicity; explicit-disorder \emph{surface} free energies
in DFT (on the SQS supercells of Stage A$'$) are the natural next computational step, beyond the
MLIP-level surface screen reported here. These limitations motivate, rather than undermine, the
experimental campaign of Chapter~\ref{ch:experiment}, which is the arbiter of the predictions.

\section{Outlook}
Beyond confirming the six candidates, the pipeline supports an active-learning loop in which
experimental and higher-level DFT data recalibrate the composition proxies and the MLIP,
tightening successive generations. The same economic-conditioned generator could be retargeted to
CO\textsubscript{2}-to-formate or to multi-carbon products by changing the conditioning
descriptors.

\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis built and applied an end-to-end generative pipeline that designs \emph{affordable}
high-entropy carbide catalysts for the CO\textsubscript{2}-to-CO reaction by embedding an economic
constraint directly into a property-conditioned diffusion model and validating the results through
a funnel of a machine-learned potential and first-principles DFT. From 1024 generated structures,
six platinum-group-metal-free high-entropy carbides emerged, each predicted to host a
near-thermoneutral CO site with CO-over-hydrogen selectivity, and all confirmed by DFT to be
metallic with a hybridized carbon-$p$/metal-$d$ conduction character. A complete experimental
validation program---synthesis, electrochemical CO\textsubscript{2}RR, on-line gas analysis,
\textsuperscript{1}H NMR, and in-situ FT-IR---has been designed to test these predictions, and its
results will complete the work. The broader contribution is a reusable demonstration that
affordability can be a \emph{generative} design objective for catalyst discovery.
